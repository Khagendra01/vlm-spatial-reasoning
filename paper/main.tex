\begin{abstract}
Aggregate spatial accuracy can hide sharply different capabilities within vision--language models (VLMs). We study object-relative orientation as a diagnostic case. On Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR), scaling from a compact model to a 7B VLM substantially improves overall, depth, horizontal, and containment accuracy, but orientation changes only modestly. Except for structured prompting, which degrades performance, trained and scaled conditions remain in a narrow 62--66\% orientation band. We test structured prompting, general and targeted LoRA, hard negatives, visual/projector adaptation, frozen and object-grounded representation probes, and object-centric decomposition; none reliably removes the bottleneck, and clean-label analysis shows that annotation ambiguity does not explain it away.

Accuracy also fails to capture relational coherence. LM-side adaptation significantly improves consistency over zero-shot, while hard-negative training raises facing/facing-away consistency from 66.0\% to 77.7\% with facing accuracy unchanged at 68.9\%; the additional pooled consistency gain over LM-only is not significant. On SITE, the 7B model is strong on the official spatial-relationship category, while a non-official orientation/direction subset is substantially weaker. VSR-trained LoRA does not improve that subset and significantly degrades the official category. The results point to a persistent orientation bottleneck involving both weakly accessible orientation representations and incomplete relational coherence, while also showing that task-specific spatial adaptation can transfer poorly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spatial reasoning is central to multimodal systems that must interact with physical environments. A useful VLM should not only recognize objects, but determine whether one object is in front of another, whether it is facing another, whether two objects are parallel, and whether equivalent or complementary formulations yield logically compatible answers. These abilities matter for robotics, embodied agents, navigation, and scene understanding.

Existing work already shows that spatial relations are difficult for vision--language systems. VSR was designed as a controlled natural-image probe and reported particularly poor performance on object-orientation relations in earlier VLMs~\cite{liu2023vsr}. More recent controlled benchmarks likewise find that strong VLMs can fail on basic left/right and other grounded relations~\cite{kamath2023whatsup,rajabi2024gsrbench}, while broader evaluations continue to identify spatial orientation as a difficult factor~\cite{wang2025site}. Our question is therefore not whether spatial reasoning can fail, but whether the orientation weakness persists in modern generative VLMs after scaling and targeted intervention, and what the failure signature reveals about representation, reasoning, and transfer.

In our VSR experiments, the distinction between relation families is striking. Scaling from \modelsmall{} to \modellarge{} increases zero-shot overall accuracy from 74.0\% to 80.9\%. Horizontal reasoning rises from 70.2\% to 84.8\%, depth from 68.9\% to 75.2\%, and containment from 83.4\% to 89.3\%. Orientation, by contrast, moves only from 62.8\% to 63.5\%. We then evaluate interventions spanning prompting, language-side adaptation, targeted data construction, visual-side adaptation, representation probing, and explicit object-centric decomposition. Structured prompting significantly hurts performance; general and targeted LoRA improve aggregate accuracy but do not remove the orientation ceiling; hard negatives improve targeted relational coherence without a significant overall orientation-accuracy gain; and visual-side LoRA fails to outperform the language-side control.

The effect survives clean-label auditing. On the strictest audited orientation subset, the best accuracy reaches 78.5\%. Because this subset is not directly comparable to other relation families, we do not treat the absolute gap to containment or depth as a matched statistical comparison; rather, the important result is that removing questionable examples raises scores but does not make orientation uniformly solved across conditions.

A second failure mode emerges from logical consistency. The zero-shot 7B model is consistent on only 36.9\% of complementary facing/facing-away pairs. LM-side LoRA substantially improves coherence, and hard-negative training raises facing consistency to 77.7\% while facing accuracy remains unchanged at 68.9\%. The extra hard-negative gain is not significant in the pooled strict-family McNemar comparison, but the unchanged accuracy together with changed facing consistency demonstrates that the two metrics capture different behavior.

Finally, we test external transfer. On SITE, \modellarge{} performs strongly on the official spatial-relationship-reasoning category (75.1\% raw; 59.2\% chance-adjusted accuracy). A transparently defined, non-official orientation/direction subset is much weaker (47.3\% raw; 22.6\% chance-adjusted).\footnote{A recomputation of the secondary-subset metrics from the committed prediction CSV using the frozen-protocol ID list yields $n{=}2{,}024$ examples at 48.3\% raw / 24.0\% CAA, whereas the run-time metrics JSON and the canonical site table report $n{=}1{,}824$ at 47.3\% / 22.6\%; all other SITE numbers are bit-identical across the two artifact paths. Both values support the same conclusion; see supplementary \S E.} VSR-trained LoRA does not improve this subset and significantly degrades the official category by 3.5 percentage points. We therefore do not claim broad SITE spatial weakness; instead, the external result supports a narrower orientation-related difficulty and exposes benchmark-specific adaptation.

Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Fine-grained diagnosis across scale and adaptation.} Across ten VSR conditions and two model scales, several spatial families improve substantially while object-relative orientation remains unusually resistant.
    \item \textbf{Robustness and mechanism analysis.} Clean-label auditing, frozen/object-grounded probes, visual-side adaptation, and object-centric decomposition constrain purely annotation-based or single-component explanations.
    \item \textbf{Accuracy--coherence separation.} Complementary-relation tests show that relational consistency can change substantially while task accuracy remains fixed.
    \item \textbf{Cross-benchmark transfer analysis.} SITE shows strong official spatial-relationship performance but weak orientation-related performance, while VSR-trained LoRA transfers poorly and significantly harms the official SITE category.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Spatial reasoning benchmarks.}
VSR introduced a controlled natural-image benchmark of more than 10k image--text pairs spanning 66 spatial relations and explicitly highlighted orientation relations such as \emph{facing} and \emph{parallel} as difficult for the VLMs studied at the time~\cite{liu2023vsr}. Our work should therefore be read as a modern diagnostic extension rather than the first observation of an orientation weakness. What'sUp isolates basic spatial relations by holding object identities fixed while varying configurations, and found large gaps between humans and tested vision--language models~\cite{kamath2023whatsup}. GSR-Bench extends controlled grounded spatial evaluation across a larger set of multimodal models and model scales~\cite{rajabi2024gsrbench}. SITE broadens evaluation to single-image, multi-image, and video inputs and organizes tasks using cognitive-science dimensions including spatial visualization/orientation, intrinsic/extrinsic relations, and static/dynamic settings; its authors report spatial orientation as a persistent challenge~\cite{wang2025site}. Recent broad evaluations such as SpatialScore similarly argue that spatial intelligence remains fragmented across tasks and modalities~\cite{wu2026spatialscore}.

\paragraph{From spatial perception to spatial reasoning.}
Recent work increasingly separates failures of perception, grounding, and reasoning rather than treating spatial ability as one scalar score. VSP decomposes visual spatial planning into finer perception and reasoning subtasks and reports deficiencies in both~\cite{wu2025vsp}. MM-Spatial focuses on 3D spatial understanding, including relationship prediction, metric estimation, and grounding, and shows that specialized spatial data and geometric inputs can improve multimodal models~\cite{daxberger2025mmspatial}. These studies motivate our relation-family and mechanism-level analysis. Our focus is narrower: object-relative orientation in natural images, with controlled tests of whether scaling, language-side adaptation, visual-side adaptation, or explicit decomposition changes the bottleneck.

\paragraph{Adaptation and spatial specialization.}
We use LoRA, which adapts frozen models through trainable low-rank updates~\cite{hu2021lora}, to compare general, targeted, hard-negative, projector, and vision-plus-projector interventions. This design lets us ask whether benchmark gains reflect general spatial competence or specialization. The external SITE transfer experiment is central here: VSR adaptation is neutral overall on SITE, does not improve the orientation subset, and significantly degrades SITE's official spatial-relationship category. The result complements benchmark work by explicitly measuring negative as well as positive cross-dataset transfer.

\paragraph{Representation probing and logical consistency.}
Diagnostic probes can reveal whether information is accessible from frozen representations, but probe performance must be interpreted cautiously because probe capacity can itself learn the target task~\cite{hewitt2019probes}. We therefore use linear and nonlinear probes, compare against majority baselines, add object-grounded features, and avoid claiming that weak decodability proves information is absent. Separately, recent work has begun to target spatial logical consistency directly. SAGE, for example, trains VLMs with geometric and linguistic duality operations to encourage coherent answers across transformed inputs~\cite{liu2026sage}. Our consistency analysis is diagnostic rather than a new consistency-training framework: it shows that instance-level accuracy and complementary-relation coherence can diverge, and that hard negatives can change coherence without changing facing accuracy.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Benchmarks}
\paragraph{VSR.}
We use VSR~\cite{liu2023vsr} as the primary diagnostic benchmark. The random-split test set contains 2,195 examples. Our loader exposes 64 relation labels in the evaluated split; the orientation family contains \emph{facing}, \emph{facing away from}, \emph{parallel to}, and \emph{perpendicular to}, totaling 137 test examples. All main VSR results use the same held-out test set and deterministic True/False parsing.

\paragraph{SITE.}
We use SITE~\cite{wang2025site} only for external validation and do not train on SITE. The image evaluation contains 2,591 examples: 1,368 single-image and 1,223 multi-image examples. Our primary SITE subset is the official spatial-relationship-reasoning category ($n=993$). We additionally report a transparently defined, non-official keyword-derived orientation subset ($n=1{,}824$), used only as supporting analysis. SITE results use the benchmark's native multiple-choice format and chance-adjusted accuracy (CAA) in addition to raw accuracy.

\subsection{Models and Conditions}
Our compact model is \modelsmall{}, and our larger model is \modellarge{}~\cite{wang2024qwen2vl}. We evaluate ten canonical VSR conditions: 2B zero-shot, 2B structured prompting, 2B General LoRA, 2B Targeted LoRA, 7B zero-shot, 7B General LoRA, 7B Targeted LoRA, 7B Hard-Neg LoRA, 7B Projector LoRA, and 7B Vision+Projector LoRA. LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} is used for all parameter-efficient adaptation conditions.

General LoRA uses a proportional sample across relation families. Targeted LoRA allocates most training examples to weak orientation/depth/horizontal families with rehearsal from the remaining relations. Hard-negative LoRA introduces audited complementary orientation examples, primarily facing/facing-away inversions. Vision-side conditions adapt either the multimodal projector or upper vision blocks together with the projector. The frozen experimental tag \texttt{paper-freeze-v1} records the final result set.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Tests}
For VSR we report accuracy overall and by spatial family. For SITE we report raw accuracy and CAA. Paired condition comparisons use McNemar tests when applicable. Clean-label orientation analysis reports the full orientation set and progressively stricter audited subsets. Logical consistency is evaluated by constructing complementary statements for strict relation pairs (left/right, front/behind, facing/facing-away) and testing whether the model assigns opposite verdicts as required. Parallel/perpendicular is treated separately as a soft complement because both relations can be false for oblique objects.

\section{Why Does Orientation Remain Difficult?}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\subsection{Frozen Representation Probes}
We probe frozen \modellarge{} visual representations without fine-tuning the generative model. Mean-pooled ViT and post-merger features support only weak relation decoding for facing versus facing-away, parallel versus perpendicular, and a four-way orientation task. Linear and MLP probes generally remain near majority baselines; preserving patch-level structure yields some signal for parallel/perpendicular, but that test set is small and confidence intervals are wide.

To reduce the confound that global features might infer relations without identifying the relevant objects, we additionally extract subject/reference regions and construct grounded visual features from subject, object, difference, and elementwise-product representations. These features do not produce a substantial or consistent gain. Following standard cautions about diagnostic classifiers~\cite{hewitt2019probes}, we interpret this conservatively: object-intrinsic orientation is not cleanly accessible to these simple frozen readouts, but weak probe performance does not prove that the information is absent from the network. Detailed probe tables and plots are in the supplementary.

\subsection{Visual-Side Adaptation and Object-Centric Decomposition}
If frozen representations were the sole bottleneck, directly adapting visual or projector components might recover orientation performance. It does not. Projector LoRA and Vision+Projector LoRA both reach 64.2\% orientation accuracy, below the 65.7\% LM-only control, while overall accuracy significantly degrades relative to the control. This suggests that the observed ceiling is not simply unlocked by the tested low-rank adaptation of the visual pathway.

We also test an explicit two-stage object-centric pipeline. Geometry-only and geometry-plus-visual classifiers substantially underperform the end-to-end 7B LM-only control on orientation. Its four-way relation module reaches 44--46\% cross-validation accuracy, near or below the 49.9\% majority-class baseline; uniform four-class chance is 25\%. Two-dimensional box geometry is structurally insufficient for intrinsic facing direction, and the tested region representations do not supply enough additional signal to close the gap.

\subsection{Logical Consistency Reveals a Distinct Failure Mode}
We construct complementary statements for strict relation families. A coherent model should return opposite truth values for left/right, front/behind, and facing/facing-away formulations of the same object pair. The zero-shot 7B model is consistent on only 58.0\% of left/right pairs, 57.0\% of front/behind pairs, and 36.9\% of facing pairs. Under independent random binary verdicts, expected complement consistency is 50\%; the observed facing value is below that reference point.

LM-only LoRA raises facing consistency to 66.0\% and front/behind consistency to 70.7\%; pooled strict-family consistency is significantly better than zero-shot ($p<0.0001$). Hard-negative training further raises facing consistency to 77.7\%. Facing accuracy, however, is identical for LM-only and hard-negative LoRA at 68.9\%. The additional hard-negative improvement is not significant in the pooled strict-family McNemar comparison ($p=0.29$). We therefore treat 77.7\% as a targeted descriptive improvement, not evidence of a statistically established global consistency advantage over LM-only. The key result is separability: identical facing accuracy coexists with substantially different facing consistency.

\section{External Validation on SITE}
\label{sec:site}
We evaluate the frozen 7B base model on 2,591 SITE image examples using SITE's native multiple-choice protocol~\cite{wang2025site}. The model is strong on the official spatial-relationship-reasoning category: 75.1\% raw accuracy and 59.2\% CAA. We therefore do \emph{not} claim that SITE confirms broadly weak spatial reasoning.

The supporting orientation analysis uses a transparently defined, non-official keyword heuristic selecting questions involving direction, facing, view, rotation, left/right, and parallel/perpendicular terminology. On this subset, zero-shot performance is 47.3\% raw and 22.6\% CAA, compared with 54.2\% raw and 31.1\% CAA over all image examples. Because this subset is heuristic and overlaps multiple SITE sources/categories, we use it as supporting evidence rather than an official benchmark factor. The external claim is therefore deliberately narrow: orientation/direction-heavy questions are disproportionately difficult in this evaluation, while official general spatial-relationship reasoning is strong.

We then apply the existing VSR-trained 7B General LoRA checkpoint to the exact same SITE examples without SITE training. The adapter is statistically neutral overall (54.2\%$\rightarrow$53.8\%, $p=0.66$), does not improve the orientation subset (47.3\%$\rightarrow$48.0\%, $p=0.50$), and significantly degrades the official spatial-relationship category (75.1\%$\rightarrow$71.6\%, $p=0.004$). VSR gains therefore transfer poorly and can induce negative transfer on the independent benchmark.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Orientation is not spatial reasoning broadly.}
The strongest conclusion is deliberately narrow. The 7B model is strong on several VSR families and SITE's official spatial-relationship category. The persistent weakness concerns the object-relative orientation family in VSR and an orientation/direction-heavy heuristic slice in SITE. This framing is consistent with the original VSR observation that orientation relations are difficult~\cite{liu2023vsr}, while extending it to modern generative VLMs and a much broader intervention analysis.

\paragraph{Representation and coherence both matter.}
The probes provide evidence of weak accessible decodability of intrinsic orientation under the tested readouts, while consistency experiments expose large logical failures even for relations answered above chance. Neither result alone identifies a complete causal mechanism. Together they support a mixed account in which orientation information is difficult to access reliably and relational decisions are not always propagated coherently across complementary formulations.

\paragraph{Task adaptation can specialize rather than generalize.}
General LoRA improves VSR overall accuracy substantially, but its SITE transfer is neutral overall and significantly negative on the official spatial-reasoning subset. Hard negatives improve a targeted coherence behavior while producing relation-specific tradeoffs. Benchmark-specific fine-tuning gains should therefore not be equated automatically with general spatial-reasoning improvement.

\section{Limitations}
Our main diagnostic test contains only 137 orientation examples, and individual relation subsets such as perpendicular are smaller still. Clean-label auditing reduces ambiguity but also decreases sample size. The SITE orientation subset is keyword-derived rather than an official benchmark category and is supporting, not headline, evidence. Our probing methods test accessible decodability under specific linear/MLP and pooling choices; failure to decode does not prove information is absent. Visual-side experiments use LoRA rather than full end-to-end retraining. We study two model scales rather than a broad model family, and the external validation is image-only; SITE video reasoning remains future work. Finally, several interventions are evaluated as negative results, so lack of improvement should be interpreted for the tested configurations rather than as impossibility claims about all prompting, adaptation, or architectural methods.

\section{Conclusion}
Aggregate spatial accuracy hides a persistent orientation bottleneck in the VLMs studied here. Scaling and standard adaptation substantially improve several spatial families while leaving object-relative orientation comparatively resistant. Auditing, probing, visual-side adaptation, and decomposition constrain simple explanations, while complementary-relation tests show that accuracy and coherence are distinct axes of spatial competence. External SITE evaluation further shows that VSR-specific adaptation transfers poorly and can negatively transfer to official spatial-relationship reasoning. The results motivate spatial evaluation that reports relation-specific performance, transfer, and logical coherence rather than relying on aggregate accuracy alone.
