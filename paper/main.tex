\begin{abstract}
Can training an answer-relevant object-pair spatial state in a
vision--language model (VLM) to obey a predeclared, geometry-derived
transformation algebra deliver measurable downstream transfer beyond matched
augmentation, output-consistency, and invariance controls? We test this
with a one-seed falsification pilot on synthetic plan-view scenes rendered
for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. We use a manipulation-verified implementation
with per-epoch structural-loss logging and a nonzero-loss assertion, and
compare six matched arms sharing the same answer path while differing only
in data or structural objective. The result is a
clean separation. When the loss is actually applied, the latent
\emph{complies} with the predeclared algebra: on a held-out transform
composition never seen in training, EquiOrient's equivariance error is
\eqnVTwoCRhoVHEq\ vs.\ \eqnVTwoCRhoVHAug\ for augmentation-only (a
$\approx$\eqnVTwoCRatio$\times$ reduction), and the
per-transform correct-vs-wrong contrast discriminates sharply
(\eqnVTwoCRhoHEq\ vs.\ \eqnVTwoCWHEq\ on the horizontal reflection), with
a wrong-geometry control that obeys \emph{its own} (incorrect) law
per-transform. Yet no measurable downstream transfer appears: the behavioral
composition test is uninformative by construction (the task reduces to a
binary left/right axis on which the answer algebra is closed, so every
arm reaches ceiling), and a held-out relation-family probe (depth) is
flat across arms (0.53--0.55). The paper is a mechanistic negative:
representation-level algebra compliance is achievable and measurable,
but it did not transfer to behavior or to an unseen relation family
within Phase-1 scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Answer-level consistency laws are a standard diagnostic: a model that
flips its answer when an image is mirrored is ``using'' the visual
evidence~\cite{liu2023vsr}. But a model can obey an answer law while its
\emph{representation} transforms arbitrarily, and vice versa. EquiOrient
tests the stronger object: can a typed, answer-relevant spatial latent be
trained to obey a predeclared, geometry-derived transformation algebra, and
does that compliance pay off behaviorally or in transfer to unseen
structure?

This paper's central contribution is the honest answer to both parts,
obtained with a discipline that the first attempt lacked. Our first
implementation---and, unknowingly, every GPU run built on it---had
identically zero structural losses: the equivariance term fabricated
$\rhoop(T)\zblk$ from the same $\zblk$ instead of coupling $\zblk(x)$ and
$\zblk(Tx)$ from the original and transformed images. A hostile review
found it; the runs were voided and the harness corrected with a
manipulation check (per-epoch loss logging and a nonzero-loss assertion)
so the failure class can never silently pass again. The corrected pilot
provides a valid controlled test, and it produced a sharply interpretable
result:
\emph{the equivariance objective works at the representation level and
fails to deliver measurable downstream transfer in the tested scope}.

Concretely, on a held-out composition $V\circ H$ never seen in training,
EquiOrient's latent equivariance error is \eqnVTwoCRhoVHEq\ against
\eqnVTwoCRhoVHAug\ for augmentation-only and \eqnVTwoCRhoVHWrong\ for the
wrong-geometry control---yet the wrong-geometry control obeys its own
incorrect law per-transform (\eqnVTwoCWHWrong\ on $H$), while EquiOrient
violates that wrong law (\eqnVTwoCWHEq). The correct-versus-wrong contrast
is therefore measured, not assumed. Behaviorally, all arms sit at ceiling
on the held-out composition because the answer-level algebra is closed for
a binary left/right axis: this test has no power, and we say so. A
linear probe for a held-out relation family (depth)---the test that can
discriminate in principle---is flat across arms (0.53--0.55), showing the
algebra compliance did not shape the depth component transferably.

We release the protocol, harness, committed matrices, and a reproducible
execution pipeline that runs the full six-arm pilot in minutes.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item a \emph{manipulation-verified} six-arm falsification harness for
transformation-equivariance training, including the per-epoch structural-
loss check that catches identically-zero implementations;
\item a controlled measurement showing that EquiOrient achieves strong,
specific, two-regime latent algebra compliance (including on a held-out
composition) without measurable downstream transfer in scope;
\item per-transform and wrong-law causal metrics that separate correct
from wrong actions where composition metrics cannot;
\item released protocol, manipulation-verified harness, committed result
matrices, and a reproducible execution pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
The novelty gate (frozen 2026-08-13) screened 30 close or foundational
works; the top threats and our differentiation:

\emph{Latent group representations.} The Homomorphism AutoEncoder
(HAE)~\cite{keurti2023hae} learns group-structured latent representations
with homomorphism-based composition from observed transitions. EquiOrient
does not claim to learn an action group---the geometry action is
predeclared---and its question is VLM-specific: whether a constrained
spatial action on an \emph{answer-relevant} pair state improves
compositional spatial reasoning beyond matched behavioral baselines.

\emph{Representation-level geometric supervision.} GASP (Beyond 3D
VQAs)~\cite{yeh2026gasp} injects 3D spatial priors into VLMs via
correspondence invariance and depth consistency. EquiOrient instead asks
whether relation-relevant \emph{components} should transform
non-uniformly under the same intervention, and whether the composed
algebra transfers to unseen transforms---with a wrong-action causal
control GASP does not have.

\emph{Transformation-pair consistency.} SAGE~\cite{sage2026} trains VLMs
to stay logically consistent under paired geometric and linguistic
operations (output/reward-level). EquiOrient treats such consistency as a
matched baseline and asks whether enforcing the structure on the
answer-path state yields additional generalization; our output-consistency
arm provides a SAGE-style output-level consistency control; our per-transform latent metrics extend it to the
representation level.

\emph{Consistency vs.\ accuracy.} Consistent Yet Wrong~\cite{cyw2026}
shows multi-view consistency can coexist with wrong answers (static
analysis of pretrained models). We perform a controlled fine-tuning
transition and measure the equivariance axis as a training outcome; our
result---algebra compliance without transfer---extends their static
finding to a causal setting.

\emph{Equivariant representation learning} (Cohen and
Welling~\cite{cohen2016}, Bronstein et al.~\cite{bronstein2021}) is
foundational; EquiOrient's contribution is the VLM answer-path
instantiation with a typed heterogeneous algebra and matched controls.
Additional screened neighbors (chain-of-spatial-thought, 3DThinker,
GeoWorld-VLM)~\cite{cosat,3dthinker,geoworld} lack the predeclared
pair-state algebra or its compositional test.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Typed spatial state and algebra}
The pair encoder maps pooled deepstack features of two objects to a
512-dim typed latent $\zblk=[\zblk_h;\zblk_v;\zblk_d;\zblk_{\mathrm{orient}}]$
($4\times128$); the relation head is $W_{\mathrm{rel}}[\zblk_h;\zblk_v]$
with forced decoding to left/right/above/below. Predeclared actions
(geometry only, never the relation label): $\rhoop(H)$ flips $\zblk_h$,
$\rhoop(V)$ flips $\zblk_v$, $\rhoop(V\circ H)=\rhoop(V)\rhoop(H)$, depth
and orientation invariant. The algebra is verified executably (21 unit
tests; 10{,}880 law checks on the scene pack, 0 violations; hostile
renderer-bug test).

\subsection{Six matched arms}
All arms share the identical answer path and trainable set (vision LoRA
on fused qkv/proj/c\_fc/c\_proj; text backbone and lm\_head frozen) and
start from a common initialized state restored per arm. Arms: (1)
ordinary SFT/LoRA, original images only; (2) augmentation-only, H/V
pairs, answer loss; (3) output-consistency, answer loss + soft answer-law
consistency $\mathrm{MSE}(\mathrm{logits}_{Tx},\,
\mathrm{perm}_{\mathrm{law}(T)}(\mathrm{logits}_x))$ with detached law
targets; (4) latent-invariance, answer loss +
$\lVert\zblk(x)-\zblk(Tx)\rVert^2$; (5) EquiOrient, answer loss +
$\lVert\rhoop(T)\zblk(x)-\zblk(Tx)\rVert^2$; (6) wrong-geometry, the same
form with the \emph{wrong} action (H acting on $\zblk_v$). Structural
losses couple the identity-image state $\zblk(x)$ with the
transform-image state $\zblk(Tx)$ on the same object ids (identity
features computed once per scene per step, gradients preserved). The
structural loss magnitude is logged per epoch and asserted nonzero
(manipulation check). The $\lambda\in\{0.1,1.0,10.0\}$ grid is shared by
all structural arms and selected on the fixed validation slice only;
wrong-geometry uses EquiOrient's selected $\lambda$.

\subsection{Held-out tests (single, post-selection, per arm)}
(i) behavioral composition $V\circ H$ on hold-out scenes; (ii) the same
with $\zblk$ zeroed (causal ablation; the head's bias decode on balanced
binary labels is 0.5 by construction---we report it as a plumbing check,
not chance); (iii) paired both-correct; (iv) latent equivariance error
$\lVert\rhoop(V\circ H)\zblk(x)-\zblk(Tx)\rVert^2$ plus per-transform
errors under the correct and the wrong rho (the composition metric alone
cannot separate correct from wrong rho: the axis-swapped wrong laws
compose to the correct action on $V\circ H$); (v) the depth probe: a
logistic regression on $\zblk$ (full and $\zblk_d$-only) fit on
validation-scene depth pairs, scored on hold-out-scene depth pairs.

\section{Protocol}
Plan-view synthetic scenes (320$\times$320), margin-guaranteed strict
relations, distinct depths, 5 objects (3 rects + 2 lines); 17 scenes
split 10/4/3 by scene id; identity, H, V seen in training; $V\circ H$
never. Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct at a frozen revision, transformers==4.57.6,
bf16/sdpa; AdamW lr 1e-4, batch 8, 2 epochs, gradient checkpointing;
one-seed Phase-1 falsification scope. The task is effectively binary:
every ordered pair carries a strict horizontal relation, so the label
rule assigns left/right and never above/below---we disclose this and
report the behavioral composition test as uninformative by construction.

both scene regimes.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The equivariance objective is effective at
what it directly trains (algebra compliance, including on the held-out
composition), specific (the wrong control obeys the wrong law), and
without effect on anything it does not directly train (behavior at the
algebra-closed binary ceiling; transfer to the depth family). This is the
mechanistic negative the design was built to find: \emph{representation
fidelity improved, downstream behavior did not follow}.

\section{Discussion}
The corrected design turns the earlier null into an interpretable
mechanistic finding. Three points deserve emphasis.

\emph{The composition test alone cannot validate an algebra.} Because the
wrong law is the axis-swapped law and $V\circ H$ is symmetric under the
swap, both correct and wrong training compose to the same action on the
composition (0.045 vs.\ 0.026). Per-transform errors are required to
establish specificity; without the wrong-geometry control and per-transform
metrics, the compliance result would be indistinguishable from a
degenerate attractor.

\emph{Algebra closure bounds the behavioral test.} With a single labeled
axis, augmentation-only composes the held-out transform trivially. Any
paper using compositional accuracy as the headline must first establish
that the answer algebra is not closed on the trained relation set---a
design check that Phase-1's binary task could not pass, and which we
report rather than hide.

\emph{Scope of the negative.} One seed, two epochs, synthetic plan-view
scenes, one backbone. The depth probe's $n{=}60$ gives limited power
(95\% CI for 0.5333: 0.41--0.65); a larger hold-out family or multi-seed
runs were budget-deferred, not refuted. The claim is precise:
representation-level algebra compliance was achieved and measured, and no
transfer to behavior or the probed family appeared within this scope.

\section{Conclusion}
With a manipulation-verified implementation, we find that training an
answer-path latent in a VLM to obey a predeclared geometry-derived
transformation algebra achieves strong, specific algebra compliance
(including on a held-out composition, $\approx$\eqnVTwoCRatio$\times$
below the augmentation-only and output-consistency controls), while
delivering no measurable downstream transfer on the algebra-closed
behavioral test or a held-out relation family probe. The full protocol,
harness, committed matrices, and a reproducible execution pipeline are
released; the design is re-testable in harder regimes in minutes.
