% ============================= Abstract =============================
\begin{abstract}
Spatial reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs) has improved dramatically, yet
\textit{object-intrinsic orientation} --- deciding whether ``the cat is facing the
dog,'' or whether two objects run parallel or perpendicular --- remains a persistent
failure. We report a causal decomposition of this bottleneck across a single
evaluation protocol on the VSR benchmark and two model families (SmolVLM2-2.2B and
Qwen2-VL-7B). Scaling barely moves orientation accuracy ($62.8\% \rightarrow
63.5\%$), and we find that the failure is jointly representational and
behavioral: frozen vision features encode object-intrinsic direction only weakly
(linear, MLP, patch-vote, and object-grounded probes all hover at chance), and
neither vision-side adaptation (projector/upper-vision LoRA, both \emph{significantly
worse} overall) nor explicit two-stage object-centric decomposition ($55.1$--$58.3\%$
vs.\ $65.7\%$ control) recovers it. In contrast, language-side training repairs
\emph{logical consistency} without changing accuracy: hard-negative training raises
facing-pair self-consistency from $36.9\%$ to $77.7\%$ at identical accuracy
($68.9\%$), showing coherence and accuracy are separable failure dimensions. A
preregistered external validation on SITE (ICCV 2025; 2{,}591 images) confirms the
weakness generalizes across benchmarks: official spatial-relationship reasoning is
comparatively strong ($75.1\%$ raw / $59.2\%$ chance-adjusted), while
orientation-vocabulary questions collapse ($48.3\%$ raw / $24.0\%$ chance-adjusted).
The bottleneck is specific to object/direction orientation, and it resists scale,
prompting, LM-side and vision-side fine-tuning, representation probing, and explicit
decomposition alike --- a targeted, reproducible target for the community.
\end{abstract}
