\begin{abstract}
Benchmark accuracy is the standard currency for reporting spatial fine-tuning of vision--language models (VLMs), yet an accuracy gain alone does not say what an adaptation actually changed: the same update may alter how strongly the model relies on the correct image, how coherently it applies relational semantics, and how it responds to a causal image transformation---independent components moving in different directions at different rates. We introduce a preregistered evaluation protocol that measures spatial fine-tuning as a jointly reported vector of capability changes rather than a scalar score: benchmark gain \dA{}, correct-image dependence \dG{} (the normal-minus-shuffled-image accuracy gap relative to zero-shot), semantic pair consistency \dC{} (complement and facing-antonym flip-law compliance), and Tier-C behavior under global horizontal reflection (transformed accuracy, pair consistency, joint correctness). We apply the protocol to ordinary LoRA fine-tuning on Visual Spatial Reasoning as a controlled intervention, across two backbone families with 3 fresh training seeds each (plus committed seed-0): \dA{} is positive for every seed on both backbones (7B: $+0.0506\pm0.0046$; 2B: $+0.0316\pm0.0009$), \dG{} is positive for every seed (7B: $+0.0440\pm0.0054$; 2B: $+0.0316\pm0.0026$), while fresh-seed pair consistency stays within 0.05 of the legacy checkpoint in both families and all fresh 2B answer-update rates exceed the 2B zero-shot rate. A post-confirmatory extension on the contemporary \qwennew{} backbone reproduces the primary \dA{}/\dG{} pattern ($+0.032$/$+0.036$) and the transformed-accuracy gain ($+0.045$). Ordinary spatial fine-tuning is therefore not a scalar capability update: benchmark accuracy, evidence dependence, and semantic consistency must be measured---and reported---as one vector whose components replicate across seeds and families. The frozen protocol (conditions, metric definitions, and independently recomputed audit scripts) accompanies this paper so that future spatial-adaptation results can be decomposed and compared on equal terms.
\end{abstract}
