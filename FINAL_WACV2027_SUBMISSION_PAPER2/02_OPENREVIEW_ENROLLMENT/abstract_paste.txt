Fine-tuning a vision-language model (VLM) on a spatial reasoning task usually improves benchmark accuracy. But "better accuracy" is not a single capability: the same adaptation may change how much the model depends on the correct image, how coherently it applies relational semantics, and how it responds to a causal image transformation, in different directions and at different rates. We treat ordinary LoRA fine-tuning on the Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR) task as a controlled intervention and measure, per training seed and across two backbone families, a vector of capability changes: benchmark gain Delta A, correct-image dependence Delta G (the normal-minus-shuffle accuracy gap, relative to zero-shot), and semantic pair consistency Delta C (relcomp/facing-antonym flip-law compliance), together with Tier-C transformation behavior under global horizontal reflection (transformed accuracy, pair consistency, joint correctness). Across 3 fresh training seeds per family (plus the committed seed-0), Delta A is positive for every seed on both backbones (7B: +0.0506 +/- 0.0046; 2B: +0.0316 +/- 0.0009) and Delta G is positive for every seed (7B: +0.0440 +/- 0.0054; 2B: +0.0316 +/- 0.0026). Fresh-seed semantic pair consistency stays within 0.05 of the legacy General checkpoint in both families, and all fresh 2B answer-update rates exceed the 2B zero-shot rate. A post-confirmatory extension on the contemporary Qwen3-VL-8B backbone reproduces the primary Delta A/Delta G pattern (+0.032/+0.036) and the transformed-accuracy gain (+0.045). We conclude that ordinary spatial fine-tuning is not a scalar capability update: benchmark accuracy, correct-image dependence, and semantic consistency can be described -- and must be reported -- as a jointly measured vector whose components replicate across seeds and families.
